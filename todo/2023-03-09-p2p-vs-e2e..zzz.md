End-to-end, point-to-point, and hop-by-hop networks are different types of network architectures. In end-to-end networks, data is sent from one end of the network to the other end, with each node in between simply acting as a relay. This means that the data passes through many intermediate nodes before reaching its destination. This is commonly used in the internet, where data is broken up into packets and then sent across the network through a number of intermediate nodes before arriving at its destination. In point-to-point networks, data is sent directly from one node to another node without passing through intermediate nodes. This type of network is often used in telecommunications, where data is transmitted between two endpoints without any intermediate relays. In hop-by-hop networks, data is sent from one node to the next closest node until it reaches its destination. This means that the data passes through fewer intermediate nodes than in end-to-end networks, but still more than in point-to-point networks. This type of network is commonly used in wireless networks, where data is transmitted from one device to another via a series of intermediate nodes until it reaches its final destination.Overall, the main difference between these network architectures is the number of intermediate nodes that the data passes through before reaching its destination.

## E2E

Reliability: End-to-end networks are highly reliable because they use many intermediate nodes to ensure that data packets are delivered to their destination. If one node fails, the data can be rerouted through a different path, making the network more resilient.

Scalability: End-to-end networks are highly scalable because they can handle a large number of nodes and devices. As more devices are added to the network, the network can adapt and adjust to accommodate the additional traffic.

Flexibility: End-to-end networks are very flexible and can be used for a wide variety of applications, including voice and data communication, video streaming, and web browsing.

Disadvantages:

Latency: End-to-end networks can suffer from high latency, which refers to the time it takes for data to travel from one end of the network to the other. This can be a problem for real-time applications, such as video conferencing or online gaming, where delays can cause significant issues.

Security: End-to-end networks can be vulnerable to security threats, such as hacking or data breaches, because data is transmitted through many intermediate nodes. This can make it difficult to protect the network from attacks.

Complexity: End-to-end networks can be complex to design and manage, especially as the number of nodes and devices on the network grows. This can make it challenging to troubleshoot and resolve issues when they arise.

Overall, end-to-end networks are a reliable and flexible option for many applications. However, they can suffer from latency issues and may require additional security measures to protect against threats. Additionally, managing and troubleshooting end-to-end networks can be complex, and may require specialized skills and expertise.


## Point-to-point
A point-to-point connection directly links system 1 (the point of payment card acceptance) to system 2 (the point of payment processing). A true P2PE solution is determined with three main factors:

The solution uses a hardware-to-hardware encryption and decryption process along with a POI device that has SRED (Secure Reading and Exchange of Data) listed as a function.
The solution has been validated to the PCI P2PE Standard which includes specific POI device requirements such as strict controls regarding shipping, receiving, tamper-evident packaging, and installation.
A solution includes merchant education in the form of a P2PE Instruction Manual, which guides the merchant on POI device use, storage, return for repairs, and regular PCI reporting.
End-to-end
End-to-end encryption as the name suggests has the advantage over P2PE that card details are not unencrypted between the two endpoints. If the endpoints are a PCI PED validated PIN pad and a POS acquirer, there is no opportunity for the card details to be intercepted. It is obviously important that the endpoints (the PED and gateway) are provided by PCI accredited organisations.

PCI point-to-point encryption requirements
The requirements include:

Secure encryption of payment card data at the point of interaction (POI),
P2PE validated application(s) at the point of interaction,
Secure management of encryption and decryption devices,
Management of the decryption environment and all decrypted account data,
Use of secure encryption methodologies and cryptographic key operations, including key generation, distribution, loading/injection, administration, and usage.[citation needed]
